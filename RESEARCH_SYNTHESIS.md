# TEMPLATE — Research Synthesis Response

Use this template when the user asks you to summarize, consolidate, or synthesize information across multiple sources, topics, or perspectives.

**Trigger conditions:** "summarize", "what does the research say", "synthesize", "compare the views on", "give me an overview of", "what's the state of"

---

## Template

```markdown
## [Topic]

### Headline Finding
[1–3 sentences. The single most important takeaway from the synthesis.
If the reader stops here, they have the answer.]

---

### Scope of This Synthesis
- **What's covered:** [Topics, time period, domains, types of sources]
- **What's not covered:** [Explicit exclusions — what's out of scope]
- **Source types:** [e.g. "peer-reviewed studies", "practitioner reports",
  "official documentation", "model's general knowledge"]

---

### Areas of Consensus
[What's widely agreed upon? State the consensus view and, briefly, why
it holds — the evidence or reasoning behind it.]

- **[Consensus point 1]** — [Brief support]
- **[Consensus point 2]** — [Brief support]
- **[Consensus point 3]** — [Brief support]

---

### Areas of Disagreement
[Where do credible sources diverge? Present each position fairly.
Avoid false balance — if one view is significantly better supported,
say so.]

**Position A — [Label]**
[Summary of the view and who holds it. What evidence supports it?]

**Position B — [Label]**
[Summary of the view and who holds it. What evidence supports it?]

**Where the weight lies:** [State, honestly, which view has more
support — or whether the question is genuinely open.]

---

### Open Questions / Gaps
- [What the available sources don't yet answer]
- [What would need to change for the picture to shift]

---

### Practical Takeaways
1. **[Takeaway 1]** — [What this means for the reader's decision or context]
2. **[Takeaway 2]** — [What this means for the reader's decision or context]
3. **[Takeaway 3]** — [What this means for the reader's decision or context]

---

### Confidence & Limitations
- **Confidence in consensus items:** [High / Moderate / Low, with reason]
- **Confidence in disagreement framing:** [How well I can characterize the debate]
- **Known blind spots:** [What I may be missing — time cutoff, niche subdomains, etc.]

---

*Want me to go deeper on any of these positions, or narrow the scope to a specific sub-question?*
```

---

## Usage Notes

- **Lead with the headline finding**, not with methodology. The reader wants the answer; the scope section justifies it.
- **Scope is mandatory.** Synthesis without a stated scope is opinion in disguise.
- **Separate consensus from disagreement explicitly** — do not mix them into a single narrative.
- **Avoid false balance.** If 95% of credible evidence points one way, do not present a fringe counter-view as equal in weight. Say so plainly.
- **Name the gaps.** A research synthesis that claims to be complete is almost certainly wrong.
- **Apply calibrated confidence** (see `UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md`) — especially for disputed or fast-moving topics.

## What to Avoid

```
✗ Presenting one source's view as "the research" without scope
✗ False balance — treating fringe and mainstream positions as equivalent
✗ Burying uncertainty — omit hedges entirely or overuse them
✗ Listing sources without synthesizing them (that's a bibliography, not a synthesis)
✗ Skipping the "Practical Takeaways" — synthesis that doesn't help the reader
  act is incomplete
✗ Claiming citations or studies you cannot actually ground
```
